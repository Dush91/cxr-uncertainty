Deep learning systems for chest radiography fail silently: a confidently wrong prediction is indistinguishable, from its output alone, from a confidently right one. This dissertation asks whether such confident errors can be detected, and builds an agentic system around the answer.

Four agents are delivered on a leak-free, architecture-diverse ensemble of pretrained chest X-ray encoders: an error-detection agent combining ensemble disagreement, temperature scaling, conformal prediction and feature-space density; a similar-case retrieval agent over a 129,113-case nearest-neighbour index; and two language agents, for per-case explanation and population-level improvement suggestion, whose every claim is mechanically audited against a computed evidence pack.

The principal contribution is a measurement. On a small benchmark, feature density appeared to flag confident errors at AUROC 0.87; re-measured on a corpus with 34 confident errors rather than five, it fell to 0.53, and ensemble disagreement proved statistically indistinguishable from pooled confidence (DeLong p = 0.99). Rather than report a failed hypothesis, this work built the missing experimental cells. A fifteen-model from-scratch control reproduced the literature's positive result, but an architecture-diverse from-scratch ensemble, recombined from existing predictions at no additional compute, reproduced the deflation exactly (0.5585 against 0.5626) despite the opposite training regime. The governing factor at high selectivity is therefore architectural diversity, not training regime; adapting the ensemble to its deployment corpus restores the effect (0.679 against 0.542, p = 0.006). Feature-space Mahalanobis distance is further shown to be the only score tested that detects confident errors under distribution shift, conformal coverage to hold in-distribution and void under shift, and flag-keyed retrieval to raise neighbourhood relevance from 0.014 to 1.000; the faithfulness audit rejected both language-model renderings produced in a live trial.

The result is a usage rule not previously available: use pooled confidence to decide whether to abstain, and ensemble disagreement to decide which confident prediction is wrong — the latter only where members share an architecture or the ensemble has been adapted in domain.
