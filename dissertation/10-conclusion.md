# Conclusion

This dissertation set out to determine whether a chest X-ray classifier's confidently wrong predictions can be detected, and to build an agentic system around whatever detector the evidence supported. The system was built and is described in Chapters 3 and 6 to 9. The detection question was answered, and the answer is more precise than the one the project set out to find.

The work began from a compelling intuition: a confident prediction on which several independently trained models disagree deserves review, because confidence is symmetric between confidently-right and confidently-wrong while disagreement, as a second-order quantity, might not be. On a 2,012-image leak-free split that intuition looked supported, and a feature-density score looked better still at AUROC 0.87. Both figures rested on five to eight confident errors. On a corpus with 34, the density score fell to 0.53 and cross-member disagreement proved statistically indistinguishable from pooled confidence at DeLong *p* = 0.99, with a single ensemble member's confidence beating the ensemble outright.

Rather than report that as a failed hypothesis, the work built the experimental cells that bound it. A fifteen-model from-scratch control reproduced the literature's positive result, which suggested the deflation was a property of frozen foundation encoders. But an architecture-diverse from-scratch ensemble — constructed by recombining predictions that already existed, at no additional compute — reproduced the deflation almost exactly (0.5585 against 0.5626) despite occupying the opposite training regime, which overturned that attribution. At the operating point that matters, the governing factor is **architectural diversity, not training regime**; at broader selectivity the reverse holds. And adapting the pretrained ensemble to the deployment corpus restored the effect (0.6789 against 0.5417, *p* = 0.006), establishing that the original deflation was a data-shift artefact rather than a property of ensembles.

The resulting usage rule is simple and was not available before this work: **use pooled confidence to decide whether to abstain, and disagreement to decide which confident prediction is wrong** — and expect the latter to work only if the ensemble's members share an architecture or the ensemble has been adapted to the data it is deployed on.

## Evaluation against the objectives

**O1 — establish whether disagreement improves confident-error detection at power under a leak-free protocol. Achieved, with a negative answer for the deployed configuration.** On the 8,082-image leak-free evaluation split, disagreement scores 0.5626 and pooled confidence 0.5630, with DeLong *p* = 0.99 and McNemar *p* = 0.98. The protocol requirements were met in full: patient-disjoint splits, contaminated weights excluded by assertion, and a corpus leak-free by recency. The answer is not the one the project was built around, and the objective is nonetheless met — establishing that a widely assumed signal is absent in a common deployment configuration is the result.

**O2 — determine whether regime or diversity governs that answer. Achieved.** The four-cell decomposition of Section 5.5, replicated at two member counts, isolates architectural diversity as the driver at the top decile (a shift of +0.08 to +0.16 from varying diversity against −0.004 from varying regime) and training regime as the driver at broader selectivity. The mechanism proposed — same-architecture cross-seed disagreement tracks data ambiguity, cross-architecture disagreement tracks inductive-bias difference — accounts for both. This is the dissertation's principal contribution and, to the best of the literature reviewed, is not reported elsewhere.

**O3 — determine whether feature density and conformal prediction add what disagreement cannot. Achieved, with a clear boundary.** Mahalanobis distance on frozen self-supervised features is the only score tested that beats chance under distribution shift (0.818 and 0.649 on 244 and 313 confident errors), and it strengthens monotonically with selectivity. It is *not* an in-distribution confident-error flagger: it sits near chance in-distribution in both training regimes. Mondrian conformal prediction holds its coverage guarantee to within two percentage points in-distribution across all thirteen valid pathologies and voids under shift, dropping to 0.25 while continuing to auto-read at a referral rate of 0.03. The two layers are complementary rather than redundant, and the boundary between them is now measured rather than assumed.

**O4 — produce a retrieval mechanism keyed on the flag, and quantify the difference. Achieved.** Keying retrieval on the Region-B-flagged class rather than on the prediction's argmax raises the share of returned neighbours whose report confirms the flagged finding from 0.0142 to 1.0000, at a cosine cost of 0.63 to 0.49. A dedicated lane for no-finding queries repaired a regression that enlarging the library had introduced (0.7396 to 0.6740 to 1.0000), and the posterior-agreement rerank raised label-Jaccard at 8 from 0.353 to 0.415.

**O5 — produce explanations and improvement hypotheses that are mechanically verifiable, and measure how often unconstrained output fails. Achieved.** Both language components ground every claim in an addressable evidence pack and pass output through a zero-tolerance audit. The deterministic path passes on 50 of 50 cases for Agent 3 and 5 of 5 lanes for Agent 4; an adversarial rendering fails with exactly the three expected checks. In the live trial, the audit rejected both renderings the model produced within the time limit — one for a fabricated number, one for four concatenated invalid citations together with five ungrounded values. The claim that mechanical grounding is necessary rather than decorative is therefore evidenced rather than asserted.

**O6 — integrate the four agents into a deployable interface and evaluate against the brief. Achieved**, with one gap. The application runs all four agents on a CPU laptop with explicit typed couplings, and Section 9.7 evaluates it against the brief's four outcomes. The gap is the brief's simulated clinician rating of explanation usefulness, which was not conducted; faithfulness was measured instead, and these are different properties.

## Reflection on process

The project's plan was a twelve-week schedule following the brief: baseline classifier, uncertainty module, retrieval, explanation, suggestion, integration, evaluation. The delivered work follows that spine but diverges in one consequential way, and the divergence is the part worth reflecting on.

At the point where the plan called for moving on from the uncertainty module, the evaluation was returning a top-decile AUROC on eight confident errors. The plan-following action was to record the number and proceed. The action taken was to stop, acquire a corpus seven times larger, and re-measure — which returned a result that invalidated the favourable numbers already in hand. That decision cost several weeks and required labelling 129,113 free-text reports, obtaining GPU time and re-running the entire battery. It is the single decision that determines whether this dissertation reports a contribution or an artefact.

Two further judgements followed from it. The from-scratch control was built because a negative without a control is unbounded and therefore uninformative. And the cross-backbone recombination was built because the control was confounded — noticing that regime and diversity had both changed, and that the missing cell could be assembled from predictions already on disk at zero cost, is the cheapest experiment in this dissertation and the one that produced its main finding.

Not everything was well managed. Two corpus problems were absorbed rather than anticipated: a set of out-of-distribution training images was evicted from a cache between two evaluation phases, which confounded one member-count comparison beyond repair; and the corpus selected for the expert-uncertainty-label test became unavailable mid-project when its government prohibited use, leaving Task 2 permanently tentative. Pinning and verifying corpora on disk before a comparison, rather than after, is the lesson. The application-facing work also revealed defects late — the argmax-keyed retrieval failure of Section 6.4 was found by looking at the demonstration, not by any metric, which suggests the evaluation battery should have included an end-to-end case inspection much earlier.

## Future work

**Complete the design.** One cell of the regime-by-diversity matrix is untested: a same-architecture *pretrained* ensemble, which would require several independently pretrained initialisations of one foundation backbone. The two straddled axes both point toward diversity at high selectivity, but this cell would settle the attribution directly, and it is the first thing to do.

**A shallow-ensemble arm.** Baur's second method — one shared backbone with several diverse heads — is a much cheaper source of disagreement than a deep ensemble and sits at an interesting point on the diversity axis: shared inductive bias, different decision functions. The mechanism proposed in Section 5.5 makes a prediction about it that could be tested directly.

**An expert-uncertainty corpus.** Every Task 2 result here rests on labels a rule-based tool inferred from report hedging. A corpus with native expert uncertainty annotations would convert a tentative finding into a real one.

**A reader study.** Faithfulness is measured; usefulness is not. The correct instrument is a study in which readers assess flagged cases with and without the retrieved neighbourhood and the generated explanation, measuring both decision quality and time. Section 9.7 records this as the brief's one unmet evaluation item.

**Act on a suggestion and re-measure.** Agent 4 identifies threshold placement as the reachable lever and reports that nearly half the error mass is threshold-reachable. Acting on that and measuring the effect requires a third held-out corpus, to avoid the split-discipline violation Section 8.3 was built to prevent — which is exactly why it was not attempted here.

**A pixel-reading adjudicator.** The design decision in Section 7.1 to keep the language model away from pixels was made for auditability and governance. A grounded vision-language adjudicator, subject to an equivalent mechanical check against a segmentation or detection backbone rather than against a numeric evidence pack, would raise the capability ceiling without abandoning verifiability. It remains proposed and unbuilt.

## Closing

The system this dissertation delivers does what the brief asked: it detects likely errors, retrieves comparable cases, explains itself and proposes improvements, on a laptop, from public models and data. The finding it delivers is narrower and more useful than the system: that the ensemble disagreement signal so widely assumed to flag confident errors is, in the architecture-diverse pretrained configurations now being deployed, indistinguishable from the pooled confidence anyone already has — unless the ensemble is adapted to the data it will run on, in which case it returns. That is a smaller claim than the project set out to make, it is bounded by controls that were built rather than assumed, and it is the claim the evidence supports.
